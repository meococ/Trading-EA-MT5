## 3. Answers to the six questions

### Q1 — Where do stop-loss and take-profit orders cluster?

**EVIDENCE (Osler 2003).** In ~9,700 conditional orders (>$55bn) at a large dealing bank, Aug 1999–Apr 2000, covering USD/JPY, GBP/USD **and EUR/USD**:

- **Take-profit orders cluster *at* round numbers** — most strongly at prices ending in "00", and materially at "50". Requested TP rates hug the round figure itself.
- **Stop-loss orders cluster *just beyond* round numbers** — stop-loss *buys* concentrated just *above*, stop-loss *sells* just *below* the round figure. Stops also cluster just beyond swing extremes (they are placed "behind" the level the holder expects to hold).
- The two asymmetries differ by side: SL-buy and SL-sell distributions are not mirror images, and TPs dominate the order mass sitting *at* the round number.

Osler's mechanism: TPs are placed by position-holders who think in round numbers (order placement is a human act — Goodhart & Curcio's early 1991 LSE FMG work on quote clustering, and Mitchell's reviews, document the same digit preference in FX quotes). Stops are placed *behind* the level for two reasons: (i) a stop at the level itself fires on a mere touch or a defensive probe — placing it beyond ensures the level actually failed; (ii) advisors/dealers conventionally recommend "stops behind support," reinforcing the offset. Result: a round level is **fronted by counter-trend depth (TPs) and backed by pro-trend depth (stops)** — a partially reflecting barrier that, once passed, detonates stored pro-trend flow. That is precisely the "magnet until passed, then accelerator" structure. Osler & Savaşer (2011) add that such price-contingent orders account for over half of FX's excess return kurtosis.

### Q2 — Do trends accelerate after a level is crossed (cascades)?

**EVIDENCE (Osler 2005), with honest magnitudes.** On 1-min quotes for USD/DEM, USD/JPY, GBP/USD (Jan 1996–Apr 1998, NY hours), defining "reaching" a round number as within 0.01% (~1 pip on a 1.xx rate): for all three pairs, post-crossing trends were significantly faster. Headline number: USD/DEM moved an average **0.061% in the 15 minutes after crossing a round number vs 0.054% after crossing an arbitrary number** — bootstrap significance below 0.001, consistent in 51 of 58 ten-day intervals. The SL-driven response is larger *and* longer-lasting than the TP-driven response; results hold for **hours, not days**, and there is evidence of "waves" (crossing one round number propagates toward the next). Two honest cautions: (i) the *mean* excess is small (~0.7bp in 15 min) — the phenomenon lives in the tail, where cascades compound; (ii) 1990s indicative quotes. Post-2010 corroboration that the mechanism survives: the BIS forensic report on the 7 Oct 2016 sterling flash event (~9% in <40 s, early Asian hours) names stop-loss execution through key levels plus option-hedging flow as amplifiers, in thin time-of-day liquidity.

### Q3 — Do reversals concentrate at round numbers?

**EVIDENCE, but as probability shifts, not a clean pip figure.** Osler (2000): published S/R — 95.5% ending in 0 or 5 — predicted intraday trend interruptions for all six firms, surviving a bootstrap against 10,000 arbitrary level sets, with predictive power lasting ≥5 business days. Sopranzetti & Datar (2002): transaction/quote digits cluster on 0/5, more so in high volume and volatility. Mitchell & Izan (2006) report psychological-barrier effects in FX rates. Osler (2003) supplies the mechanism (TP wall at the level). **What the literature does NOT give is a per-event pip-size reversal expectation at M5 scale** — effects are documented as increased interruption/reversal frequency and faster post-crossing speed. The pip-size conversion is a measurement we must run ourselves (§6).

### Q4 — Do these effects survive after ~2010?

**Mixed — and the framing matters.** (a) *Signal-level TA profits:* Neely, Weller & Ulrich (2009) show simple-rule FX alpha was gone by the early **1990s** — before algos; Hsu, Taylor & Wang (2016) show residual predictability persists into the 2010s but is regime- and market-dependent. (b) *Microstructure phenomena:* unambiguously alive post-2010 — Krohn, Mueller & Whelan's fix reversals run through 2019; Evans (2018) documents fix-window anomalies across 2007–2017; Chaboud et al. (2014) show algos made EBS *more* efficient while humans still carry the informed flow; BIS (2017) shows cascades still detonate in thin sessions. **Conclusion: order-clustering physics (levels, fixes, seasonality) survived; chartist-rule alpha decayed.** An engine that *perceives* structure (where latent flow sits) is on the surviving side of that line.

### Q5 — Intraday seasonality and our session objects

Documented shape (Dacorogna et al. 1993; Andersen & Bollerslev 1998; Ito & Hashimoto 2004/2006 on EBS EURUSD & USDJPY; Krohn et al. 2024):

- **Volatility/activity follow the geographic workday** — three humps (Asia, Europe, America), dips at Tokyo and London lunch, late-NY fade; *no* end-of-day surge in NY (Ito & Hashimoto).
- **Scheduled spikes (GMT winter → CET):** Tokyo fix 00:55 GMT = 01:55 CET; US data 8:30 ET = 14:30 CET (ABDV: conditional-mean jumps); NY option cut 10:00 ET = **16:00 CET** (EBS volume spike documented); ECB fix 14:15 CET (Krohn et al.: ~2.5bp pre-fix drift then revert); London fix 16:00 London = **17:00 CET** — *our clock note says "16:00 CET winter"; that is the London-local time, one hour early — flag for the calendar module.*
- **Asian session (00:00–08:00 CET):** the low-activity trough — consistent with treating the Asian range as *balance*. Berger et al. (2008) warn the corollary: order flow bites hardest when liquidity is lowest — so Asian-range edges, though quiet, are exactly where a push produces outsized displacement (BIS sterling event is the extreme case).
- **EU open (08:00–09:00 CET = Frankfurt/London ramp):** activity jumps at each center's open — consistent with "first imbalance" of the liquid day. Breedon & Ranaldo add a small directional prior (EUR tends to soften in European hours, firm in US hours).

### Q6 — Range formation: balance, absorption, liquidity provision inside ranges

**EVIDENCE-adjacent.** Kavajecz & Odders-White (2004, NYSE): S/R levels coincide with depth peaks, and causality runs from existing depth to the level — liquidity providers post depth *at* technical levels because that is where counterparties expect to trade. Translated to FX via Osler (2003): TP depth at round levels + limit depth at box edges means a range is a region bounded by *two-sided* resting flow; inside it, mean-reverting liquidity provision dominates (depth peaks at edges). Sopranzetti & Datar's volatility–clustering link implies the *price grid* inside a box tightens in quiet regimes. "Absorption" of an attack on a box edge = TP/limit depth consuming initiator flow — observable on bars only as repeated rejection with shrinking follow-through; "a level weakens as its depth is consumed" follows from the depth-mechanics view (each touch executes part of the book), which is the *opposite* of the folk "stronger with each touch".

## 4. What is NOT established

- **"A level gets stronger with each touch."** No FX evidence; mechanism evidence points the other way (depth is consumed when hit — Kavajecz & Odders-White; Osler's orders execute and disappear). FOLKLORE.
- **"The third touch is the best / nth-touch rules."** No empirical paper found testing touch-count monotonicity in FX. FOLKLORE. Osler (2000) found even the publishing firms' own strong/weak labels had zero predictive content.
- **"False breakout" as a defined, tested phenomenon.** Not directly studied. It is *implied* by Osler's TP-at-level / SL-beyond-level asymmetry (stop trigger → absorption → retrace) but no paper quantifies false-break frequency or size on intraday FX. Mechanism = EVIDENCE-backed; the named pattern = PRACTITIONER.
- **"NY 10:00 ET option cut pins EURUSD to strikes."** Volume spike: EVIDENCE (Ito & Hashimoto EBS data; Ito & Yamada). Price pinning in FX: no dedicated study found — nearest rigorous evidence is equity pinning (Ni, Pearson & Poteshman 2005, ≥16.5bp) plus the delta-hedge mechanics in Wystup (2025, PRACTITIONER). Grade: PRACTITIONER with an evidence-grade mechanism.
- **"Barrier options pin/defend spot at round-number barriers."** Mechanism documented (delta > notional near barrier; hedging slows approach, unwind accelerates breach — Wystup; Osler & Savaşer list barrier hedging inside price-contingent flow). An FX-specific empirical pinning estimate was not found. PRACTITIONER + THEORY.
- **Level "strength scoring" generally.** No validated ex-ante ranking scheme in the literature — explicitly falsified for analysts' own labels in Osler (2000).

## 5. Limitations

- **PDF parsing.** `webfetch` returned binary for journal/Fed PDFs (Osler 2000 EPR, FRBNY SR150, Osler 2003 WP). All metadata and headline numbers were verified from official landing pages, abstracts, and search-extracted text of those same documents; a few in-table figures (e.g., Osler 2003's full digit distributions, exact stop-offset distances in pips, Osler 2000's per-firm interruption frequencies) were **not** extracted — flagged rather than guessed.
- **[SNIPPET-ONLY, NOT FETCHED]:** Mitchell & Izan (2006, DOI verified but text not fetched); Goodhart & Curcio (1991, LSE FMG discussion paper, seen only via Sopranzetti & Datar's reference list); Westerhoff (2003) on FX psychological barriers (seen only cited in a later paper); Krugman (1991, QJE) target-zone model cited from standard knowledge as THEORY; Niederhoffer & Osborne (1966, JASA) cited via Osler (2003)'s text; Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron (1992, JF 47(5)) cited via Neely et al.'s reference list.
- **Era skew.** The deepest evidence (Osler's order data, Evans & Lyons, Andersen & Bollerslev, Sopranzetti & Datar) is 1996–2000, DEM-era or infancy-of-euro, indicative quotes, one bank. EBS-era studies end ~2007. Post-2010 evidence exists for fixes, seasonality and cascades (Krohn et al. to 2019; Evans 2018; BIS 2017) but **no published study re-runs Osler's order-clustering work on 2012+ EURUSD** — our own order-structure priors must be treated as mechanism-informed, not re-measured.
- **Frequency skew.** Survival results (Neely et al.; Hsu et al.) are daily-bar studies; extrapolating to M5 is ours to test.
- The GBP flash event is GBP, not EUR; the pinning evidence is equities; Kavajecz & Odders-White is NYSE — each used as mechanism, not direct EURUSD evidence.

## 6. What this means for our engine

All primitives are bars-only and causal (bars ≤ t). Clock is CET/CEST.

**P1 — Level object = zone, not line.** Justification: Osler's "reaching" tolerance was 0.01% (~1 pip at 1.30) and stops sit *just beyond*. Set zone half-width = max(3 pips, 0.75 × M5 ABR(20)) asymmetrically: the *far* side of a level (where stops sit) extends ~2–5 pips further than the near side. Provenance: Osler 2003/2005 (0.01% reach threshold; beyond-clustering), magnitude partly our choice — literature gives the asymmetry, not the exact pip offset → **measure: histogram distance-from-round-number of M5 swing extremes and of post-break stall points on our 2012+ data.**

**P2 — Round-number grid.** 00/50 as primary; 25/75 as secondary in low-ABR regimes — *not* the reverse. Sopranzetti & Datar: clustering *rises* with volume/volatility, so coarse grid matters most when active; Osler (2000): 95.5% of analyst levels end 0/5. In quiet Asian tape a finer grid is defensible only after measuring digit-clustering on our own ticks (bar-close last-digit histogram) — literature gives no M5-era EURUSD digit distribution → **self-measure required.**

**P3 — Magnet-then-accelerator state machine.** On approach into a level zone: expect stall/absorption (reflective TP depth). On a decisive traverse (close beyond far edge, ≥ ~1.5 pips beyond zone on 2012-EURUSD scale, i.e., > 0.01–0.02%): expect elevated continuation speed for the next ~3–24 bars (Osler 2005: excess speed significant for *hours*; 15-min excess ~0.7bp mean but fat-tailed). Treat first 1–3 bars post-traverse as the cascade window; flag "wave" continuation if the next round number is within ~1 × prevailing hourly range.

**P4 — Stand-aside windows (hard calendar).**
- **14:30 CET ± 15 min** (8:30 ET US data): ABDV — conditional-mean jumps, asymmetric; Chaboud et al. — liquidity thinnest in the first post-release minute. Do not evaluate level breaks initiated inside this window as structure.
- **14:15 CET ECB fix ± 10 min** — *new item from Krohn et al. 2024*: ~2.5bp pre-fix USD drift then reversion; a V-shaped non-level event.
- **16:00 CET NY option cut ± 10 min:** EBS-documented volume spike; pinning unproven in FX → demote to "soft" stand-aside unless our own M5 study shows pinning.
- **17:00 CET (winter) / 17:00 CEST (summer) WMR fix:** the user's "16:00 CET winter" note is the London-local time — correct CET is **17:00**; window is now 5 min (since 15 Feb 2015) → stand-aside ~16:50–17:10 CET; month-end days get double weight (Evans 2018: worst anomalies month-end; Melvin & Prins: directional month-end flows).
- **Tokyo fix 01:55 CET** — inside our Asian-range definition; treat as a noise source within the Asian box, not an edge signal.

**P5 — Session objects.**
- *Asian range (00:00–08:00 CET):* lowest documented activity → model as balance; edges are stop-cluster candidates (P1 asymmetry); breakout attempts *during* Asia are low-conviction (thin liquidity → high false-break risk, Berger et al. liquidity-dependence + BIS 2017 thin-session cascades). Asian box edges gain meaning when retested after the open ramp.
- *EU open (08:00–10:00 CET):* documented activity ramp at each center's open → "first imbalance" assumption is EVIDENCE-backed. Optional sign prior: mild EURUSD-soft bias in European hours (Ranaldo 2009; Breedon & Ranaldo 2013) — small, regime-conditional; use as tie-breaker, never a gate.
- *Boxes/ranges generally:* edges = zones of expected two-sided depth (K&OW mechanism); "absorption" primitive = rejection wicks at edge + shrinking M5 ranges on successive tests (depth being consumed → level weakening, not strengthening).

**P6 — Explicitly not implemented from folklore:** touch-count scoring, nth-touch rules, fixed "the level is strong" ratings — Osler (2000) falsified ex-ante strength labels; depth-consumption logic implies monotonic *weakening* per touch.

**P7 — Measurements we must run ourselves** (literature gives direction, not M5 EURUSD numbers): (i) reversal/interruption frequency vs distance-to-level and vs round-number grid on 2012+ M5; (ii) post-traverse speed distribution (mean *and* 90th percentile — cascades live in the tail); (iii) per-15-min CET volatility/volume curve to recalibrate all windows in *our* feed's timezone; (iv) false-break rate and retrace size after edge excursions of 1–10 pips; (v) fix-day vs non-fix-day and month-end interactions; (vi) bar-close digit clustering for grid granularity.

**Bottom line.** The perception engine's central objects — zones at round numbers and session ranges, reflection-then-acceleration at edges, scheduled stand-asides — are on EVIDENCE-grade footing as *mechanisms*. The specific pip sizes and probabilities at M5 scale in post-2010 EURUSD are not published; the engine must treat the literature as a prior and the measurements in P7 as the posterior.
