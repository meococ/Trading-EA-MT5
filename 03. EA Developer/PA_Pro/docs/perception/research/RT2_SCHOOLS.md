# RT2 — Other schools, operationally

**Sub-agent:** RT2 (PERCEPTION lane). **Date:** 2026-09-21. **Deliverable path:** `docs/perception/research/RT2_SCHOOLS.md`.

## 1. Scope, method, what was verified

**Question.** How do the major price-action schools define, draw, mechanistically explain, and *refuse to draw* the structures in our grammar — box/range, breakout, failed breakout/trap, pullback/flag, trendline/channel, double top/bottom, head-and-shoulders, squeeze/wedge/triangle, carried level?

**Method.** `web_search` to locate sources, `webfetch` to verify each before citing. Nothing cited from memory. Every claim graded:

- **EVIDENCE** — data + method + sample + result.
- **PRACTITIONER** — a named trader's untested rule.
- **FOLKLORE** — repeated without source or test.

**Honest ratio up front.** Of the substantive material below, roughly 15% is EVIDENCE (essentially all of it Thomas Bulkowski's automated pattern cataloguing on US stocks, daily bars), ~70% PRACTITIONER, ~15% FOLKLORE (mostly inside ICT/SMC and unsourced "everyone knows" rules). No school except Bulkowski publishes failure counts. No school publishes a causal, bar-by-bar specification complete enough to code without interpretation — the glossary-grade exceptions being Darvas's box rule and ICT's FVG, both of which are fully specifiable.

**Market/timeframe caveat, stated once and applied throughout.** Brooks's home market is the E-mini S&P 5-minute chart (closest to our EURUSD M5 problem). Wyckoff is 1930s US equities on dailies with real volume. AMT/Market Profile is CBOT bond-futures day sessions in 30-min brackets. Darvas and Bulkowski are daily US stocks. ICT/SMC is nominally aimed at FX/futures intraday — closest in market, weakest in verification. Every "transfers?" note below flags the gap.

**Reading the matrix.** Every school agrees on the same geometry at the coarse level: a sideways zone bounded by two horizontal-ish edges, probes beyond the edges that mostly fail, a retest of the broken edge, and a contraction before expansion. They disagree on (a) *what confirms* — Brooks: bar quality + follow-through; Wyckoff: volume; AMT: time-at-price acceptance; classical: close + measured statistics; ICT: close-back-inside + structure shift; (b) *what the edge is made of* — touch-points (classical/Brooks), events (Wyckoff), distribution shape (AMT), stop clusters (ICT).

## 4. Convergences and genuine disagreements

**Convergences (independent schools, same geometry):**

1. **The poke-and-fail is the highest-consensus structure.** Brooks's failed breakout, Wyckoff's spring/upthrust, AMT's "look above and fail"/excess, Bulkowski's busted pattern, ICT's liquidity sweep — five traditions independently describe: excursion beyond an established extreme → cannot hold → closes back inside → moves the other way. This is the single most corroborated primitive in the literature, and it is fully bar-computable (see §7, POKE_REJECTION).
2. **Retest of a broken edge.** Brooks's breakout test, Wyckoff's BUEC/LPS, AMT's acceptance test at prior value edges, the classical throwback/pullback (measured at 62–68% of patterns by Bulkowski), ICT's breaker/mitigation. A broken edge *carrying* is cross-school doctrine, not Volman idiosyncrasy — and Bulkowski quantifies the return rate on daily stocks.
3. **Contraction precedes expansion.** Tight TR/barbwire (Brooks), Phase C shrinking spread+volume (Wyckoff), narrow IB (AMT), triangle with declining volume (classical — measured: volume trends down 84–86% in symmetrical triangles), AMD consolidation (ICT). All five mark the squeeze; they differ on whether volume is required (Wyckoff: yes; Brooks: optional).
4. **Equal extremes attract.** Brooks's double top as test, Wyckoff's ST, AMT's revisited poor highs, Bulkowski's confirmation requirement, ICT's equal-highs liquidity pool. Every school treats matched highs/lows as a *magnet to be probed*, which is the opposite of the retail reading "double top = sell."
5. **Second attempt is the entry.** Brooks's H2/L2 and second entry; Wyckoff's test-of-spring; AMT's second probe with acceptance; classical 1-2-3 change (Sperandeo via Bulkowski); ICT's sweep→CHoCH→retrace sequence. "First probe fails, second probe decides" is universal.

**Genuine disagreements (reported, not smoothed):**

1. **"Most breakouts fail" vs breakout continuation.** Brooks: "80% of breakout attempts fail" out of a range; "most breakouts fail" [S2,S3]. Darvas: the box system *only* trades breakouts at fresh extremes and pyramids them — a continuation machine. Bulkowski's rectangle data splits the difference: upward rectangle breakouts fail to rise ≥10% only 15% of the time (bull markets, daily stocks) but downward breakouts fail 34% — direction and regime matter enormously [S14]. Wyckoff resolves it by phase: in Phase B the UT *should* fail; after Phase D the SOS *should* hold — the same geometry is bullish or bearish depending on where in the absorption sequence it sits. This is not a contradiction the schools would acknowledge as one; it is a disagreement about base rates that only our own labelled data can settle for M5 FX.
2. **Volume.** Wyckoff and Darvas make volume *definitional* (spring without volume signature isn't a spring; breakout on weak volume is suspect). Brooks treats volume as nearly irrelevant ("the chart in front of me"). ICT dropped volume almost entirely [S18]. Spot FX has no consolidated tape anyway — our engine must treat tick-volume versions of Wyckoff tests as a separate, testable hypothesis, not as doctrine.
3. **What a level *is*.** Classical/Brooks: a price where turns happened (touch-points). AMT: a price where *time* was spent (POC/VAH/VAL — a touch that reversed fast leaves a tail, not value). ICT: a price where *stops* sit (equal highs are more significant the more exact). These are three different primitives; on M5 bars we can compute all three and test which predicts the next reaction.
4. **Trendlines.** Classical: validate on third touch. Brooks: eyeballing is fine. AMT: doesn't draw them at all. ICT: draws them only to mark the stops beneath. Whether a trendline *as a drawn object* adds anything beyond the swing points that anchor it is a live disagreement — our CONTEXT_LINE-vs-swing-only comparison can measure it.
5. **Falsifiability of the trap.** Classical stops at "the pattern failed." Brooks, Wyckoff, AMT, ICT all claim the failure itself is signal (trap → fuel for opposite move). Bulkowski's busted-pattern stats are the only published numbers: 36% of double tops bust, and single-busted ones averaged +54% [S14] — i.e., on daily stocks the trap is real and large. Unverified for M5 FX.

## 5. What each school refuses to draw

Collected exclusion rules — the most transferable content for an engine whose failure mode is over-drawing.

**Brooks.** (a) A trading range is not broken until follow-through exists; a poke is a *test*, not a break, and may over/undershoot [S1,S2]. (b) Beginners flatly should not trade barbwire — the object exists to be *avoided*, not traded [S1]. (c) Countertrend scalps and early reversal picks are refused: a strong trend must first transition to a TR before an opposite trend is possible [S2]. (d) A second signal is preferred over a first for countertrend wedge reversals [S1]. (e) Anything requiring a close: strength is judged by where the bar *closes*, not where it probed.

**Wyckoff.** (a) No event labels without volume context — a low-volume drift to support is not a spring candidate; the volume signature is definitional [S5,S8]. (b) Refuses premature phase labelling: if the SOS can't hold, Phase C/D is relabelled back to Phase B — the framework explicitly expects relabelling ("the Wyckoff Method is always an unfolding story") [S6]. (c) Refuses to predict which side breaks in Phase B: "volatile and aimless" is a *refusal to draw conclusions* by design. (d) Rule 1: "the market is an artist, not a computer" — refuses mechanical pattern equality outright [S5]. (e) High-volume lows are *expected to be retested* — refuses to treat them as done.

**AMT/Market Profile.** (a) Refuses to honour a flat extreme: a poor high/poor low — mechanically formed, no excess — is "unfinished business" that should be revisited, i.e., *not* a durable edge [S10]. This is the school's most useful exclusion for us: an edge with no rejection tail is a weaker edge. (b) Refuses breakout calls without acceptance (price holds + time builds + volume supports) [S10]. (c) Refuses to trade non-trend days: "stay light or flat, don't force it" [S10]. (d) Refuses initiative interpretation on hollow/divergent participation [S12]. (e) Doesn't draw lines at all — the distribution *is* the object.

**Darvas/classical.** (a) Darvas: no box without a fresh extreme (new 12-month high in his version) followed by 3 sessions failing to exceed it; no trade without volume expansion on the break; refuses falling boxes for longs entirely [S13]. (b) Rectangle needs ≥2 touches each side; triangle needs ≥3+2 and price crossing side-to-side (a "filled" triangle, not white space); flag requires a steep flagpole and ≤3-week duration — else it's a different object [S14,S15]. (c) Trendlines: two points draw it, the third validates; spacing too tight or too wide invalidates; "best not to force" [S15]. (d) Patterns are only *confirmed* at the neckline/valley close — unconfirmed double tops are not double tops, and Bulkowski's 53–63% non-confirmation rate is the quantitative reason the school insists.

**ICT/SMC.** (a) A wick through a high is not a sweep — requires close back inside plus MSS/CHoCH [S17]. (b) Killzones refuse setups outside time windows — the school's one cleanly falsifiable filter. (c) An order block needs the displacement after it; without structure break it's just a candle [S17]. (d) *Systematically* weaker exclusions than claimed: the glossary itself warns liquidity can be relabelled to fit any outcome, and the critique literature demonstrates the framework can explain every result post hoc [S19]. Treat ICT's drawn objects as candidate generators whose exclusion power must come from *our* filters, not theirs.

**Meta-lesson.** Every mature school's edge against over-drawing is the same three-part gate: (i) the object must earn its boundary (touches/time-at-price/volume signature), (ii) the break must prove itself (close + follow-through/acceptance), (iii) the failure is itself an object (spring/bust/sweep). Volman's T/F labels and the "room" rule are exactly this gate.

## 7. What this means for our engine

Causal primitives — all computable from closed M5 bars at time t, no future data. Parameters carry units; provenance tagged; mapped to our object types (BOX, RANGE_OPEN, CONTEXT_RANGE, PATTERN_LINE, CONTEXT_LINE, LEVEL_CARRIED, MINI_LEVEL, SQUEEZE, LABEL_TF, BRACKET). Where schools disagree, the proposed discriminating measurement replaces the coin flip.

**P1 — POKE_REJECTION** *(serves LABEL_TF, BOX edge maintenance).* Bar trades ≥ `poke_min` pips beyond a live edge (default 1–3 pips, per Volman convention; cross-school alternative: any penetration) **and** closes back inside the box, with the close-side wick ≥ `wick_frac`·bar_range (default 0.5). Provenance: Brooks failed-breakout + Wyckoff spring + AMT excess + ICT sweep — the four-way convergent object. ICT/Bulkowski refinement: the failed-poke *pattern* is a separate event only if price then exits the opposite edge within `confirm_bars` (default ~5) — Wyckoff's "successful test" as the gate between T (tease, held) and F (failed edge → flip roles).

**P2 — EDGE_ACCEPTANCE** *(serves BOX break, LABEL_TF upgrade).* A break is confirmed only after `accept_closes` consecutive closes beyond the edge (default 1–2) **or** `accept_bars` bars traded beyond it without closing back (default 3–5). Provenance: Dalton's price+time acceptance tests [S10], Brooks's "no breakout until follow-through" [S2], classical close-past-neckline confirmation. **Discriminating measurement:** on our labelled boxes, compare P(follow-through ≥ 1.0 ABR within 10 bars) for wick-break vs close-break vs 2-close-break definitions — this directly arbitrates the schools' wick-vs-close disagreement.

**P3 — OVERLAP_DENSITY** *(serves BOX vs no-draw, SQUEEZE).* Fraction of the last `n` bars whose range overlaps the previous bar's (Brooks tight-TR/barbwire: default n≥3, density ≥0.8, ≥1 doji for barbwire flag). Provenance: Brooks glossary definitions verbatim [S1]. Use: a *candidate* congestion failing the density test is noise — do not draw; this is the school's own over-draw filter.

**P4 — VALUE_PROFILE_M5** *(serves CONTEXT_RANGE edges, POC level).* Distribute each bar's range uniformly over price buckets of `bucket` pips (0.5–1.0) across the last `session_bars` (e.g., 96 bars = 8h); POC = argmax bucket; VAH/VAL = smallest symmetric region containing 70% of accumulated volume-at-price. Provenance: Steidlmayer/Dalton TPO/VA [S9,S10]. Gives distribution-defined range edges — the only school that locates edges by *time spent*, not by touches. **Discriminating measurement:** which edge type — extreme-based (max high/min low) vs value-based (VAH/VAL) vs equal-extremes — best predicts the location of the next reaction? Run all three on the same labelled ranges.

**P5 — POOR_EXTREME flag** *(serves BOX edge quality, LEVEL_CARRIED).* An edge extreme formed by a flat cluster (≥3 bars ending within `tol` pips, no terminal rejection bar) is tagged POOR — predicted revisit probability elevated. Provenance: Dalton's poor highs/lows = unfinished business [S10]; ICT's equal highs as magnets [S16]. **Measurement:** compare revisit-within-N-bars rate for extremes with vs without a rejection tail — tests both AMT and ICT's core claim in one regression.

**P6 — EQUAL_EXTREMES** *(serves BRACKET M/W, LEVEL_CARRIED magnet).* Two swing highs/lows within `eq_tol` pips (default 1–2) separated by `sep` bars (2–30). Provenance: Brooks double top ("about the same", 1–20+ bars [S1]); ICT equal highs/liquidity pool [S16]; classical double top. **Measurement:** is poke-then-reject more likely after *equal* extremes than after a lone extreme? This is the falsifiable core of "liquidity above equal highs."

**P7 — SECOND_PROBE / H2L2 count** *(serves MINI_LEVEL trigger).* Count pullback legs against the dominant move: first countertrend swing = H1/L1, second = H2/L2 (Brooks bar-counting [S4]); Wyckoff's "test of the spring" is the same second probe [S8]; ICT's sweep→retrace sequence likewise. Draw the second probe's extreme as MINI_LEVEL; trigger = its break. **Measurement:** conditional win/behaviour rate of second-probe vs first-probe triggers on our labelled setups — arbitrates whether "second entry" is a real edge or folklore.

**P8 — MM_PROJECTION** *(serves LEVEL_CARRIED target, obstacle distance).* Target = breakout edge + `k`·range_height (k=1.0; Brooks measured move/measuring gap [S1]; classical measure rule — with Bulkowski's per-pattern "percentage meeting target" as prior: H&S 51%, rectangle up 78%, sym-triangle 58% [S14]). Also leg1≈leg2 variant. Emit as LEVEL_CARRIED obstacle, never as trade instruction. **Measurement:** on our boxes, distribution of post-break travel in units of range height — is the mode near 1.0 (Brooks) or far below (Bulkowski's small average declines for down-breaks)?

**P9 — VOLUME_EFFORT_RESULT** *(serves SQUEEZE quality, spring grading — tick volume only).* At each edge probe, compare probe-bar tick volume and bar spread to the preceding `n`-bar average: absorption signature = high volume + narrow spread (effort without result); exhaustion signature = shrinking volume + narrowing spread on the retest (Wyckoff quality test [S6,S8]). Provenance: Wyckoff; Darvas quiet-box/ loud-breakout [S13]. **Caveat:** tick volume, EURUSD — treat as hypothesis; measure whether any volume-conditioned label beats the price-only baseline before trusting it.

**P10 — BREAKER / ROLE_REVERSAL** *(serves LEVEL_CARRIED directly — our name for it is already correct).* A broken edge that price retests from the far side and fails to re-penetrate within `retest_bars` becomes a carried level. Provenance: Brooks breakout test/breakeven-stop test [S1]; Wyckoff BUEC [S6]; classical throwback/pullback — measured base rate 62–68% of confirmed patterns return [S14]; ICT breaker block [S17]. This is the single most cross-validated object in the file.

**P11 — CHANNEL_FIT** *(serves PATTERN_LINE, CONTEXT_LINE).* Line through ≥2 same-side swings; **validated** only at third touch within `tol` pips (classical 3-touch rule [S15]); spacing gate — anchors must be separated by ≥ `min_sep` bars and the gap ratio between consecutive anchors within [0.25, 4] ("not too close, not too far" [S15]); residual tolerance `resid` ≤ 0.5·ABR for a *best-fit* variant (Brooks eyeball allowance [S1]). Micro-channel variant: ≥5 consecutive bars each touching the line zone with ≤1 pullback (Brooks [S1]) — on M5, flag as momentum regime, draw CONTEXT_LINE.

**P12 — CONTRACTION_RATIO** *(serves SQUEEZE).* Over the last `n` bars (2–8): mean bar range ≤ `k`·ABR (k≈0.8, matching our existing SQUEEZE spec) **and** successive swing excursions decreasing (triangle/wedge geometry). Provenance: Brooks tight TR; Wyckoff Phase-C shrinking spread; Bulkowski's declining-volume triangle with ~74%-of-apex breakout timing [S14] gives a *duration prior*: expect resolution before the geometric apex, not at it.

**P13 — BOX_GESTATION (Darvas)** *(serves BOX birth filter).* Borrow the 3-bar hold rule as a *minimum evidence* gate on our box top: a candidate edge that hasn't survived `hold` bars (default 3) without being exceeded is not yet an edge [S13]. **Measurement:** our engine's over-draw failure mode is premature boxes — test whether a gestation rule (edge must hold N bars before drawable) raises precision more than it lowers recall on the golden set.

**Discriminating measurements summary (run on DESIGN/labelled data, not picked by doctrine):**
1. Wick-break vs close-break vs acceptance-break definitions → P2 test. (Brooks vs classical vs AMT.)
2. Equal-extreme vs lone-extreme poke outcomes → P6 test. (ICT liquidity claim, falsifiable version.)
3. Rejection-tail vs flat extremes revisit rates → P5 test. (AMT poor-highs claim.)
4. Post-break travel in range-height units → P8 test. (Brooks measured move vs Bulkowski's pattern stats.)
5. Second-probe vs first-probe trigger quality → P7 test. (Brooks/Wyckoff/ICT second-entry convergence.)
6. Volume-conditioned vs price-only spring/trap labels → P9 test. (Wyckoff vs Brooks volume disagreement, under tick-volume caveat.)
7. Base rate: fraction of our labelled box breaks that fail (Brooks's "80%" vs Darvas's continuation system vs Bulkowski's 15–34%) → the one number that decides whether our default posture after any BOX break is *fade* or *follow*.

**Bottom line.** The schools agree on geometry and disagree on confirmation currency — bar-close quality (Brooks), volume (Wyckoff/Darvas), time-at-price (AMT), statistics (Bulkowski), structure-shift (ICT). Our engine should draw the shared geometry once, tag each candidate edge with all five confirmation scores where computable, and let labelled EURUSD M5 data pick the currency. The exclusion rules in §5 — gestation, acceptance, poor-extreme tagging, "don't force the line," barbwire-as-avoidance — are each school's answer to over-drawing, and they are all cheap to compute.
