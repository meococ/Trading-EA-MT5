Q: Object: PATTERN MARKS. Q4+Q5: For double tops/bottoms, wedges, flags, squeezes and failed breakouts - when is each pattern INVALIDATED or confirmed, and what does the trader do when it fails? What is the role of the middle peak/valley in a double top/bottom? Cite sources.

### **1. Double Tops & Double Bottoms**

#### **Confirmation**
* **Confirmation Event:** A double bottom confirms as a valid chart pattern only when price rises and **closes above the peak between the two valleys** (the confirmation line / neckline) [1-4]. Similarly, a double top confirms when price drops and closes below the valley between the two peaks [5, 6].
* **Statistical Context:** Without a close beyond the confirmation line, the price structure is considered unconfirmed "squiggles on a chart" [1, 4]. Bulkowski notes that entering prior to confirmation carries a 48% chance that price will continue lower without ever confirming the double bottom [2]. Lo, Mamaysky, and Wang define double tops/bottoms as requiring the two extreme price points to be within 1.5% of their average [7].

#### **Invalidation**
* A double bottom is **invalidated** if price drops and closes below the lowest valley extreme of the pattern [8].
* For a double top, the pattern is invalidated if price rises and closes above the highest peak extreme [9, 10].

#### **Role of the Middle Peak / Valley**
* **Confirmation Price Level:** The middle peak (in a double bottom) or middle valley (in a double top) defines the **confirmation threshold** [1, 2, 4]. 
* **Target Calculation (Measure Rule):** Under classical measure rules, the vertical height is calculated from the highest peak between the two bottoms down to the lowest valley [2]. This height is then added to the breakout price (the peak) to project the price target [2].
* **Market Structure & Order Flow:** In price action theory, this middle point serves as the neckline and retest level [6]. It marks the boundary where trapped traders attempt to exit at breakeven or where new trend traders enter [6, 11].

#### **Trader Response Upon Failure**
* **When Invalidation Triggers:** If price breaches the extreme opposite of the pattern (e.g., dropping below the lowest valley of an attempted double bottom), protective stops placed a penny below the lower extreme trigger a loss exit [8].
* **Trading the "Busted" Pattern:** When a reversal pattern collapses and rejoins the primary trend, it creates a "busted double bottom/top" [12, 13]. Traders switch bias to trade in the direction of the dominant trend, capitalizing on liquidation pressure from trapped reversal traders [6, 12, 14].

---

### **2. Wedges (Rising and Falling Wedges)**

#### **Confirmation**
* **Confirmation Event:** Wedges (typically 3-push diagonal structures) confirm when price breaks decisively through the trend channel line or diagonal boundary and establishes follow-through or a successful retest [15-18].
* **Criteria:** A valid penetration requires decisive close beyond the diagonal line (often accompanied by volume or price momentum) [17-19].

#### **Invalidation**
* Wedges are diagonal chart constructions, which are prone to "morphology" (where price breaks briefly, fails to follow through, and morphs into a longer-duration pattern) [20, 21].
* A wedge is **invalidated** if a breakout attempt fails immediately and price rolls back inside the diagonal boundaries, or if price breaks out in the direction of the existing trend rather than reversing [18, 20, 21].

#### **Trader Response Upon Failure**
* **Cut Losses Quickly / Strict Risk Management:** Because diagonal structures like wedges have higher failure rates (~70% failure or fizzle rate on diagonal trendlines according to Brandt), traders avoid holding through pattern morphology and exit quickly at designated stop levels [20, 22, 23].
* **Trade Trend Resumption:** If a countertrend wedge failure occurs, price action traders look to enter in the direction of the prevailing trend as soon as the countertrend move stalls [11, 24, 25].

---

### **3. Flags (Bull Flags and Bear Flags)**

#### **Confirmation**
* **Confirmation Event:** A flag confirms when price breaks out of the flag consolidation range in the direction of the preceding impulse trend (e.g., a bull flag breaking above its upper resistance line) and achieves follow-through (such as a second leg up or a moving average / trendline breakout) [26-29].

#### **Invalidation**
* A flag setup is **invalidated** if price retraces deeply beyond the origin or swing pivot of the flag structure, or if it breaks out in the opposite direction of the expected trend continuation [30-32].

#### **Trader Response Upon Failure**
* **Fading Trapped Breakout Traders:** When a flag fails (for instance, a double bottom bull flag that breaks to the downside as a strong bear bar), bulls who bought early become trapped [32, 33]. Traders exploit this by shorting the failure, targeting a measured move down driven by the liquidation of trapped positions [11, 32, 33].

---

### **4. Squeezes (Range Contraction & Price Compression)**

#### **Confirmation**
* **Confirmation Event:** A squeeze (where price is compressed between a trendline/EMA and a horizontal boundary or within narrow-range inside bars) confirms when a strong breakout candle or cluster of signal bars closes decisively outside the compression boundary [34-38].
* **Volume Filter:** Legitimate squeeze breakouts are confirmed by expanding volume on the breakout bar [19, 35].

#### **Invalidation**
* A squeeze is **invalidated** when one side "wriggles out" of the squeeze without breaking the primary barrier, or when price produces a false "tease break" and immediately closes back inside the contraction range [34-36, 39].

#### **Trader Response Upon Failure**
* **Neutralize Bias & Shift Strategy:** If price fails to break the barrier and escapes the squeeze in the unexpected direction, traders quickly abandon the biased thesis, adjust their technical "tipping points" (stops), or trade the counter-move back across the range [34-36, 40].

---

### **5. Failed Breakouts / False Breakouts (BOF / Overshoots / Traps)**

#### **Confirmation**
* **Confirmation Event:** A failed breakout setup (also known as a Touch-Search-Test [TST] or Breakout Failure [BOF]) confirms when price probes/overshoots beyond a key support or resistance level, fails to attract follow-through buying/selling ("stretching like a rubber band"), and closes back on the "right" side of the level inside the range [41-45].
* **Candle Evidence:** Confirmation typically appears as a high-volume rejection bar (e.g., a long-wick pin bar, hammer, shooting star, or engulfing candle) closing back inside the range [41, 43, 46-48].

#### **Invalidation**
* A failed breakout setup is **invalidated** if price achieves true breakout acceptance—characterized by strong volume expansion through the level and a successful "break-and-retest" where old resistance holds as new support (or vice versa) without price returning inside the range [19, 49, 50].

#### **Trader Response Upon Failure (Trading the Breakout Failure)**
* **Trade Mean Reversion:** A confirmed breakout failure at one extreme of a range indicates that supply/demand has absorbed the move; traders enter on the open of the bar following the rejection close, expecting price to traverse to the opposite side of the range [41, 42, 44, 51].
* **Stop Placement:** Stops are placed just beyond the extreme spike/wick of the false breakout overshoot [41, 51, 52].
* **Capitalizing on Double Pressure:** Traders recognize that breakout traders are trapped in losing positions; as those trapped traders liquidate, their stop-runs generate "double pressure" that accelerates price in the reversal direction [6, 11, 33, 53].
