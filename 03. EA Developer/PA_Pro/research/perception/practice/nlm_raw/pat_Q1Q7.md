Q: You are analysing a corpus of practitioner price-action sources. Object type: PATTERN MARKS - double tops/bottoms, wedges, flags, squeezes, failed breakouts, teases. Q1+Q7: What is the MINIMUM structure that makes each of these patterns real to a practitioner (double top/bottom, wedge, flag, squeeze, failed breakout, tease)? When is each 'born' and when confirmed? Cite sources for every claim.

Across practitioner price-action literature, a technical pattern is not merely a visual visual shape; it represents a specific sequence of order-flow dynamics and trader behaviors [1-3]. Practitioners distinguish sharply between a pattern in its **formation/candidate stage ("born")** and a pattern in its **validated state ("confirmed")** [4, 5].

---

### **1. Double Top / Double Bottom**
* **Minimum Structure**: Two distinct price extremum tests (valleys/troughs or peaks) reacting near the same horizontal price level, separated by an intervening reaction peak or trough [4, 6-8]. 
  * In quantitative classical charting (Lo, Mamaysky & Wang), a double top/bottom requires an initial extremum \\(E_1\\) and a subsequent extremum \\(E_a\\) within **1.5% of their average price**, separated by at least **22 trading days** [9].
  * In empirical chart pattern studies (Bulkowski), two valleys must bottom near the same price separated by a reaction peak measuring at least 10% [4].
  * In intraday price-action scalping (Al Brooks, Bob Volman), it represents a **double test** of a swing high or low where market participants fail twice to push price further, signaling trapped traders and a potential two-leg reversal [6-8].
* **When Born**: Born on the **second test**, as soon as price returns to the price level of the first peak or valley (\\(E_1\\)) and reverses to form the second peak or valley (\\(E_2\\)) [4, 6, 9].
* **When Confirmed**: Confirmed only when price **closes decisively beyond the intervening reaction peak (for a double bottom) or trough (for a double top)**—often called the confirmation price or neckline [4, 5, 10, 11]. Without a close past this boundary, it remains "just squiggles on a chart" with a high probability (~48%) of continuation rather than reversal [4, 10].

---

### **2. Wedge (Rising / Falling Wedge, Wedge Top / Bottom)**
* **Minimum Structure**: A diagonal pattern framed by **two converging trendlines slanting in the same direction** (e.g., a rising wedge slanting upward or a falling wedge slanting downward) [5]. In price-action structural terms (Al Brooks), a wedge top or bottom is fundamentally a **three-push pattern** (three distinct legs or pushes in a narrowing channel) [12, 13].
* **When Born**: Born upon the **completion of the second boundary touch on both converging lines**, or when price prints its **third push** within the narrowing channel [5, 12, 13].
* **When Confirmed**: Confirmed when price **breaks out decisively through the boundary opposite the wedge’s slant** (e.g., breaking below the lower boundary of a rising wedge) [5, 13]. Classical chartists (Peter Brandt) emphasize that diagonal patterns are prone to "morphology" (morphing into larger consolidation structures) and are never valid until the boundary breakout occurs [5, 14].

---

### **3. Flag (Bull Flag / Bear Flag / Micro Flag)**
* **Minimum Structure**: A sharp directional trend leg or "flagpole" followed by a brief, shallow consolidation or counter-trend retracement (often containing 1 to 10 bars or a steep micro trendline) [15-17].
* **When Born**: Born as soon as the initial momentum leg pauses and forms a **counter-trend retracement or consolidation** with at least one or two small counter-trend pushes [15, 17, 18].
* **When Confirmed**: Confirmed when price **breaks out of the consolidation or micro trendline in the direction of the main preceding trend** (e.g., breaking above the resistance of a bull flag) [15, 17-19]. Technical analysts note that a flag structure means little without a confirmed breakout resumption [18, 19].

---

### **4. Squeeze (Build-up / Range Compression)**
* **Minimum Structure**: Price action compression where higher lows press against a horizontal resistance level (bullish squeeze) or lower highs press against a horizontal support level (bearish squeeze), narrowing the price range into a tight signal line or doji cluster right against the barrier [6, 20, 21].
* **When Born**: Born when price **repeatedly tests a horizontal barrier while making successive higher lows or lower highs**, showing that one side is "tightening the screws" and absorbing opposing supply/demand [20-22].
* **When Confirmed**: Confirmed when price **breaks out through the horizontal barrier or signal line**, generating "double pressure" from trapped counter-trend traders liquidating and breakout traders entering simultaneously [20-22].

---

### **5. Failed Breakout (False Breakout / Overshoot)**
* **Minimum Structure**: Occurs when price moves beyond a pre-identified support or resistance boundary/zone but lacks follow-through, immediately reversing and reclaiming the level [23-26]. Structurally, it consists of an **overshoot past a level followed by a rejection candle** (long wick/pin bar closing back on the "right" side of the level) or a bar closing back inside the range [19, 23, 27].
* **When Born**: Born at the precise moment price **pokes or probes past the support/resistance level or pattern boundary** [23-26].
* **When Confirmed**: Confirmed when price **fails to hold the broken level and closes back within the original pattern boundaries or support/resistance zone** [19, 23, 24, 27]. Peter Brandt's strict execution rule captures this confirmation: *"If a market closes back within the boundaries of a completed pattern, I cover"* [19].

---

### **6. Tease (Tease Break / Fakeout Probe)**
* **Minimum Structure**: A subtle intraday subtype of a failed breakout (Bob Volman), defined as a **tiny 1–2 pip probe beyond a barrier or signal line** that lacks volume expansion or candle size, tempting aggressive traders before immediately stalling [21, 28].
* **When Born**: Born the instant price **inches 1 pip past a drawn barrier line** without an immediate breakout impulse [21, 29].
* **When Confirmed**: Confirmed as a "tease" when the breakout bar **fails to follow through and prints small dojis or stalls at/inside the barrier**, exposing the initial break as a lure rather than a genuine move [21, 28].
