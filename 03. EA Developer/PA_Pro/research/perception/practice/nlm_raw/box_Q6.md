### **1. Chart Economy: How Many Ranges or Boxes to Keep at Once**

Across technical and price action literature, professionals emphasize **strict minimalism and clutter reduction** [1-3]:

* **The 3–5 Level Rule:** Most professional intraday and swing traders keep only **3 to 5 primary zones** (or boxes/ranges) on an active chart at any given time [2, 4, 5]. Having more than that leads to "chart clutter" and decision paralysis where every minor move hits an artificial line [2, 3].
* **The "15-Second" / Visual Clarity Test:** Veteran chartists like Peter Brandt state that if you draw more than 4 lines on a daily chart, you have drawn too many [1]. If a pattern or range takes longer than 15 seconds to identify, it is discarded [6]. Similarly, price action educators note that if you have to second-guess a level, it isn't an important one [7].
* **Active Pruning:** Professionals continuously delete broken or stale boxes [8, 9]. Once price closes cleanly beyond a range boundary with volume, that structure is retired so it does not interfere with current context [8-10].

### **2. Which Range / Box Matters NOW?**

The range that matters most in the present turn is determined by **immediate price proximity and structural context** [11, 12]:

* **The Active "Kill Zone" / Immediate Boundaries:** The primary range of interest is defined by the immediate support zone below current price and resistance zone above current price [11, 12]. 
* **Market Evolution (Spike, Channel, Trading Range):** Traders like Al Brooks evaluate how current market structure evolves [13]. For example, when a trend channel breaks down, the market transitions into a trading range, making the start of the prior channel or recent major swing points the primary active magnets [13, 14].
* **Wyckoff Phase Boundaries:** In Wyckoff analysis, the active box is defined by the outer boundaries set by the Stopping Action (e.g., Buying/Selling Climax and Automatic Reaction/Rally) [15]. Whatever phase the range is currently in (Accumulation, Reaccumulation, or Distribution) dictates how price behavior at those ceiling/floor boundaries is interpreted *now* [15, 16].

### **3. How Professionals Select Among Candidate Ranges**

When multiple valid ranges or support/resistance candidates exist, traders filter them using a clear hierarchy of criteria [8, 11, 17]:

#### **A. Higher Timeframe Ownership (Hierarchy)**
* **Higher Timeframes Rule:** A zone drawn from a weekly or daily chart always outranks an intraday (e.g., 5-minute or 15-minute) range [2, 18]. Because more market participants see higher-timeframe boundaries, significantly larger order flow clusters around them [2, 18].
* **Grading System:** Professionals categorize daily/weekly macro ranges as **"A-grade"** setup areas, whereas session-specific or intraday swings are classified as **"B-grade"** auxiliary boundaries [5, 11, 19].

#### **B. Significance & Impulse (Strength of Reaction)**
* **Sharpness of Rejection:** The validity of a range boundary depends heavily on how aggressively price moved away from it in the past [20, 21]. A level that generated a violent price displacement or trend reversal carries far higher significance than a shallow or sluggish pivot [17, 21].
* **Minimum Touch Rule:** At least **two clear prior reactions** are required to establish a valid boundary; a single touch is regarded as a guess rather than a verified level [8, 22, 23].

#### **C. Freshness vs. Degradation (The Decay Factor)**
* **Order Consumption:** Fresh ranges (tested once or twice with sharp rejections) are the most reliable [20]. As a range boundary gets tested repeatedly (3rd, 4th, or 5th touch), the resting orders sitting at that level thin out, making eventual breakout far more likely [20, 23].
* **Stale Expiration:** Academic research (such as Federal Reserve Bank studies on foreign exchange S/R levels) and professional guidelines show that S/R effectiveness decays over time [24-26]. Levels that have not been tested within 1–2 weeks lose relevance and are removed [24].

#### **D. Round Numbers & Confluence**
* **Psychological Anchors:** Statistical analysis shows that over **70% of professional S/R levels end in '0'**, and over **96% end in '0' or '5'** (such as '00' or '50' levels) [27]. Round numbers act as natural psychological magnets where institutional orders stack [28, 29].
* **Confluence Upgrades:** When a horizontal range boundary aligns with a key round number, prior session high/low, or dynamic indicator (e.g., VWAP or moving average), its quality is upgraded from fair to high-conviction [11, 30, 31].
