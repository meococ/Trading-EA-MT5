### Patterns Professionals Mark vs. Leave Unmarked

**What Professionals Mark:**
* **Obvious Horizontal Support & Resistance Zones:** Professionals prioritize major horizontal levels where price reversed multiple times, such as prior session highs/lows, overnight extremes, and major daily/weekly swing points [1-4]. 
* **Horizontal Classical Formations:** Classical chartists prioritize completed geometric structures with horizontal boundaries (e.g., horizontal Head & Shoulders, double tops/bottoms, rectangles, and cup & handles) on higher timeframes [1, 5-7].
* **Standard Trendlines & Channels:** When trendlines are used, professionals only mark standard lines connecting clear successive swing pivots (higher lows in uptrends, lower highs in downtrends) and parallel trend channels that contain price action [8-11].

**What Professionals Leave Unmarked:**
* **Diagonal Patterns & Slanted Structures:** Strict classical chartists (such as Peter Brandt) intentionally leave diagonal patterns (e.g., trendlines, symmetrical triangles, wedges, and channels) unmarked because violations carry no defined price targets and suffer higher failure rates than horizontal breakouts [6, 12].
* **Intermediate & Single-Pivot Levels:** Minor intraday fluctuations, single weak pivots, or intermediate levels inside a range are left off the chart to maintain focus on major structural levels [3, 13-15].
* **Incomplete or Forced Geometry:** Formations that do not adhere strictly to precise geometric guidelines or have not fully completed are ignored [16, 17].
* **Stale & Broken Levels:** The moment a level or trendline is broken cleanly with volume, or fails to generate a reaction over extended time, professionals **delete/prune** it rather than leaving it on the chart [13, 18-20].

---

### How Many Marks Professionals Keep on a Chart at Once

Professionals enforce strict limits on chart markings to prevent visual clutter and decision paralysis [21-24]:

* **The 4-Line Limit:** Veteran chartist Peter Brandt notes that drawing more than **four lines** on a 12-month daily chart indicates that a trader is likely over-analyzing or forcing structure onto the market [25].
* **3 to 5 Key Levels:** Intraday price action and futures trading frameworks recommend keeping **3 to 5 horizontal zones** per instrument (or 5 to 6 key zones per forex pair) at any given time [2, 23, 26].
* **1 or 2 Lines per Zone:** When marking a level, traders use either a single line for exact pip/tick reactions or two lines to mark the boundaries of an active support/resistance band [15].

---

### Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Forcing Patterns & Over-Analyzing Noise:** 
   Beginners often assume every chart must contain a tradable pattern if studied long enough, resulting in forced lines across random market noise [27-29]. In reality, valid chart patterns are rare exceptions that "pop off the page" naturally; forcing a label onto unstructured market action is a primary cause of losses [27-29].
2. **Chart Clutter (Too Many Lines):**
   Drawing 10 to 50 lines creates a scenario where price is constantly touching a line, manufacturing false trade signals and inducing hesitation on setups that actually matter [21, 22, 24, 27].
3. **Drawing Rigid Lines Instead of Zones:**
   Beginners treat support and resistance as exact 1-pixel figures rather than shaded bands [18, 26, 30]. Price routinely overshoots or wicks through exact numbers; marking the area between candle closes and wick extremes provides proper room for signals to form without breaking the trade idea [18, 26, 30].
4. **Accumulating Dead Lines:**
   Failing to delete old or broken levels turns the chart into a cluttered blueprint [18, 19]. Stale levels lose relevance as market participants change, and keeping them leads to anchor bias [19, 31].
5. **Improper Trendline Technique:**
   Common errors include drawing trendlines directly through candle bodies, connecting non-pivot points, drawing lines underneath a downtrend (or above an uptrend), or drawing lines on timeframes lacking distinct swings [32-34].
