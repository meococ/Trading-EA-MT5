### **1. Birth of a Sloped Trend Line & Anchor Swings**

* **Minimum Anchor Points**: Constructing a tentative trend line requires a **minimum of two swing points (pivots)** [1-3]. However, connecting two points merely establishes a candidate line; a **third touch or contact point is required to confirm its validity** [2, 3].
* **Kind of Anchor Swings**:
  * An **uptrend line** (demand line) must slope upward and be placed **underneath price**, connecting successive **higher lows** where buying demand steps in [4, 5].
  * A **downtrend line** (supply line) must slope downward and be placed **above price**, connecting successive **lower highs** where selling supply steps in [5, 6].
  * **Structural Sequence**: Under classical rules (such as Trader Vic and Adam Grimes), a valid uptrend line must connect a **significant pivot low to the pivot low that directly precedes the highest trend high** [7, 8]. If no distinct swing points exist on the chart, a standard trend line cannot be drawn [9].
  * **Mechanical Definitions**: Systems like **TD Lines** (Thomas DeMark) define anchor swings mechanically using "TD Points" (e.g., a Level 1 high requires a bar whose high is immediately preceded and succeeded by lower highs) [10].

---

### **2. Wicks vs. Candle Bodies**

* **Standard Wick (High/Low) Attachment**: Standard chart construction connects the extreme price points—the **wicks/tails** of swing candles—to encapsulate the full range of price rejection [4-6].
* **Candle Bodies (Closes)**: Some price action traders, such as Al Brooks, note that using **candle bodies** can sometimes yield clearer or broader trend lines [11]. Similarly, general support and resistance frameworks often prioritize candle closes/bodies as primary references because closes represent where price actually settled [12].
* **Practical Guidelines & Warnings**:
  * Rayner Teo suggests adjusting line placement to capture the **maximum number of touches**, whether they land on wicks or bodies [13].
  * However, Adam Grimes explicitly warns against drawing lines that touch candle bodies while slicing through wicks/shadows, calling it a **sloppy practice** that invalidates the line unless execution is strictly based on bar closes [14].

---

### **3. Price Violation and Line Exclusivity**

* **Must the line stay outside prices between anchors?** **Yes.** A core rule of standard trend line geometry is that the line **must not pass through or cut any prices at or between its two attachment anchor points** [8, 14-16].
* **Invalidation on Cut**: If price penetrates or cuts through a line between the two attachment points, the line is **invalidated** and loses technical significance [8, 14]. Extending broken or sliced lines into the future is considered an error [8].
* **Penetration Criteria for Subsequent Breakouts**: To test whether a later price move past the line is a valid breakout, classical rules look for criteria such as a **1% to 3% close beyond the line** or a clear surge in volume [17].

---

### **4. Channels & Parallel Return Lines**

* **Construction**: A **trend channel line** (or parallel return line) is constructed by drawing a line strictly parallel to the main trend line [18, 19].
  * **Uptrend Channel**: The primary trend line sits below price (connecting higher lows); the parallel return line is drawn **above price** and attached to a **pivot high located between the two anchor lows** [18, 19].
  * **Downtrend Channel**: The primary trend line sits above price (connecting lower highs); the parallel return line is drawn **below price** and attached to a **pivot low located between the two anchor highs** [18-20].
* **Exclusivity Rule**: The parallel return line must **not cut prices between its attachment points** when drawn [19].
* **Market Dynamics**: Pushing or overshooting past a trend channel line indicates an overbought or climactic market state [19, 21], while a break of the channel line often signals a potential reversal or transition into a trading range [20, 22].
